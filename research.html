Research

Seeds, ranges and the traces they leave

Three questions run through my work: where seeds travel, where species vanish first, and what genomes still remember of both.

Ghosts of the megafauna

Madagascar lost its giant lemurs, elephant birds and other large fruit-eating animals only a few thousand years ago. The plants they used to disperse are still there — palms with fruits far too big for any animal now living on the island. My PhD asked what that mismatch has done to them.

Combining species distributions, dispersal-related traits and genome-wide data from double-digest RAD sequencing, I found that the loss of megafrugivores is still written into today's palm distributions, into patterns of species turnover, and into the population genetics and demographic histories of individual species.

Where species disappear first

A species rarely goes extinct everywhere at once. It thins out somewhere — usually at an edge of its range — long before it is lost. In the PaDRE project I asked how predictable those edges are, by assembling national and regional Red Lists of vascular plants into one comparable dataset and matching it against ecological niches, traits, phylogenies and spatial data.

Genomes, maps and the tools in between

Much of the work is computational: population structure, admixture, bottlenecks, demographic models and phylogenetic inference on one side; species distribution models, variation partitioning, mixed models and trait imputation on the other. I work mainly in R and on the Unix command line, on high-performance computing clusters, and I care a lot about turning results into figures that actually explain something.

Fieldwork

In 2019 I spent three months collecting palm samples across Madagascar with the Kew Madagascar Conservation Centre; iDiv made a short film about the expedition. Earlier fieldwork took me electrofishing in rivers across Spain, and trapping small mammals along an altitudinal gradient in Telemark, Norway.

Methods & tools

Genomics. ddRADseq library preparation and analysis, population structure and admixture, bottleneck and demographic modelling, phylogenetic inference — Stacks, Structure, Admixture, Plink, BayesAss, BEAST, RAxML, MrBayes.

Spatial and comparative analysis. Species distribution models and ensembles, variation partitioning, linear mixed-effects models, trait and genetic data imputation, GIS.

Lab and field. DNA extraction, PCR and real-time PCR, gel electrophoresis, microsatellites; planning and leading fieldwork, collecting herbarium specimens, identifying plants in the field.

Computing. R and Unix (bash) on high-performance computing clusters, some Python, and a lot of care put into figures and maps.
