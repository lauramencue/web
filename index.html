Evolutionary ecology · Macroecology

Dr. Laura Méndez Cuéllar

Why do plants grow where they do — and what stays behind in their genomes once the animals that carried their seeds are gone?

I am a biologist working between evolutionary ecology, macroecology and population genomics. My research combines spatial and genomic data to ask how biodiversity is distributed, and how it changes — across landscapes, and across both deep and very recent time.

Since 2025 I have been a postdoctoral researcher and lecturer in the Biodiversity of Plants group at the University of Marburg. Before that I worked at the Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research (UFZ) in Halle on PaDRE, a project on the patterns and drivers of regional plant extinctions, and completed my PhD at the German Centre for Integrative Biodiversity Research (iDiv) Halle-Jena-Leipzig on palms with large fruits in Madagascar.

Along the way I helped build a molecular lab, led fieldwork in Madagascar, taught master's courses and supervised students.
